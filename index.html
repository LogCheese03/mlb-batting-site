    About the data

    Source

    The Lahman Baseball Database, compiled by Sean Lahman and maintained with SABR. It is free to download as CSV files. This project uses six of its tables: Batting, Pitching, People, Fielding, Teams and TeamsFranchises, from the 2025 release (covering 1871–2025, released January 2026). The 2026 regular season comes from MLB's public Stats API, and the Matchups tab uses Retrosheet play-by-play files; both are described below.

    What one row is

    One row is one player's batting line for one team in one season (a "stint"). A player traded mid-season has one row per team. The file used by the site has rows and columns covering seasons (–) and franchises.

    Pitching table

    The pitching half uses a sixth Lahman table, Pitching. One pitching row is one pitcher's line for one team in one season (a "stint"), the same grain as the batting rows. The file has rows and columns covering the same seasons and franchises. Seasons before are excluded ( rows) and row with no batters faced is dropped. A pitcher is a starter for a given team and season if he started at least half of his games, otherwise a reliever. Innings are stored as outs and divided by 3, so no rounding enters the totals. Holds are not recorded in the Lahman data, so saves are the only late-inning count shown.

    Correlations and park factors

    The "correlation" in the home run, strikeout and ballpark findings is the Pearson correlation coefficient: 1 means two numbers always rise and fall together, 0 means no linear relationship, and negative numbers mean they move in opposite directions. The home run and strikeout correlation uses the league-wide rates for each of the seasons (and, separately, for each decade and for each year-to-year change). The ballpark finding uses each franchise's average park factor from the Lahman Teams table (BPF, where 100 is a neutral park and a higher number favors hitters) for franchises, and compares it with the franchise's ERA and home runs allowed per 9 innings. Lahman publishes park factors through , so the 2026 season is not in the park averages. A correlation shows that two things move together, not that one causes the other.

    The 2026 season

    Lahman publishes a season only after it ends, so its 2025 release stops at 2025. The 2026 regular season, which ended September 27, was added from MLB's public Stats API by scripts/fetch_mlb_2026.py (fetched UTC). That is hitting lines and pitching lines, one per player and team, cleaned with the same rules as every other season ( hitting lines with no plate appearances are dropped). Each MLB player is matched to his Lahman playerID by birth date and name, so careers continue across the two sources: of players matched, and the rest, mostly 2026 debuts, keep an MLB id. Primary position is the position with the most games, with left, center and right field combined as Outfield, as in Lahman. Postseason games are not included in any season. As a check, the player rows were summed by team and compared with MLB's own published team totals: of comparisons match exactly, and the only differences are in , because a team's earned runs are not always the sum of its pitchers' earned runs.

    Matchup data (Matchups tab)

    Hitter-versus-pitcher results are not in Lahman, which only has season totals. From through they come from Retrosheet's play-by-play event files, read by scripts/build_matchups.py. Every plate appearance is classified from its play record (single, double, triple, home run, walk, hit by pitch, strikeout, sacrifice, or other out) and credited to the pitcher who was on the mound according to that game's lineup and substitution records. Retrosheet has not published 2026 yet, so that season comes from MLB's own play-by-play feeds (scripts/fetch_mlb_2026_pa.py): completed regular-season games and plate appearances, with players matched to the same ids as the older seasons. Every pairing is kept, with no minimum number of plate appearances: players, hitter-pitcher pairs and plate appearances in season rows. Because that is too much to load at once, the page keeps one small file per hitter and one per pitcher and loads the one you pick. Season totals built this way are within of this site's hitting file from 1970 on. Retrosheet is missing some 1960s games, so those seasons run up to low. As a check, each player-season was compared with the hitting and pitching files: hits, home runs, walks and strikeouts match exactly for of hitter-seasons and of pitcher-seasons; most misses are a single walk, because official scoring can charge a walk to the pitcher who started the count. The information used here was obtained free of charge from and is copyrighted by Retrosheet. Interested parties may contact Retrosheet at 20 Sunset Rd., Newark, DE 19711.

    Rows dropped and cleaning

    Seasons before are excluded ( rows) to keep the study to the modern era. Rows with zero plate appearances are dropped ( rows) because those players never batted. Any missing counting stat is treated as zero; in this release cells were missing, so no values changed. Team codes are mapped to franchises so that, for example, the Montreal Expos and Washington Nationals count as one group. rows have no fielding record and are labeled "DH / pinch hitter."
